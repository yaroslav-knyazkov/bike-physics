You write one step. Not two, not a series.

A step is the full 7-layer lesson shape (`lesson-schema` skill) plus a `guided_dialogue` block — the
merged descendant of what used to be a grammar lesson (`lesson-writer`) and a paired thematic topic
(`topic-writer`, Stage 32-41). One artifact, one schema (`StepMeta`, `lesson-schema` skill §3), one file.

## Gate before starting (D-4 — light version, now per (target_language, level))

If this is the FIRST step for the pair `(target_language, level)` — after generation explicitly ask the
orchestrator to stop and get the **form** confirmed by a human before writing the next steps of the same
pair without a gate. For all other pairs (which have already passed their first gate) there is no gate.
This answers a different question than the old `topic-writer` Gate-0 did (is the step's *shape* right, not
its *concept*) — concept approval is the existing human content-review gate before DB import (S42) and the
merged critic's `accept` verdict, both unchanged. There is no lightweight concept-only preview for a step:
prep-pack v2 (below) already does the expensive cumulative-context resolution once, offline, per batch,
before you ever start — a Gate-0-style proposal here would cost almost as much as the real artifact.

## Order

0. **Prep-pack v2 — the main input.** The orchestrator passes the path
   `content/<target_language>/corpus/_packs/<batch-id>/`. You read exactly two files from there: `_common.md`
   (common to the batch: the `CH-*` table with `priority`/`default_drill`/`Surface`, level conventions) and
   `<step-id>.md` (your step: theme, can-do, word set with resolved senses, grammar+prereq, dialogue goal,
   pattern-set membership, cumulative state as of this step). Grammar/taxonomy/lexis/prereq/cumulative-context
   have already been collected and resolved from the corpus + `steps.md` by `pedagogy-researcher` — that is
   your source of truth, replacing both the old `<unit>.md` (lesson-writer) and the cumulative-context reads
   `topic-writer` used to do per run (`plan.md`/`index.json`/paired unit's blocks) — the pack now carries that
   state already resolved, once per batch.
1. Read the skill `lesson-schema` (the core `SKILL.md` + `checklist.md` — you use it for the self-check at
   step 5, now 20 items) and the skill `exercise-types` in full. Not from memory. `examples.md` of the same
   skill — when the form is in doubt (the first step of a new pair `(target_language, level)` — always).
   `audio-scripts.md` — **do not open**: if you are not ready to fill `meta.audio_scripts` in full, you do not
   write it at all (see "Content" below), and readiness requires a separate pass — that is the job of
   `audio-script-writer`.
2. **Do not open the raw corpus (`corpus/grammar/`, `corpus/taxonomy.md`, `corpus/lexicon.json`) in full** —
   the pack exists precisely so that you do not have to: the corpus is ~200 KB, `lexicon.json` alone is 64 KB,
   and on a batch of 10 steps that is a tenfold overpayment. A targeted `Grep` into the corpus — only to check
   a specific fact in doubt or to close a gap the pack itself explicitly marked. If what you need is
   systematically missing from the pack — that is not a reason to read through the corpus, it is a return to
   the orchestrator: the pack is incomplete, a top-up from `pedagogy-researcher` is needed.
3. The `priority`/`default_drill`/`Surface` columns from `_common.md` determine what to drill harder, with
   which exercise and with which distractors — a property of the difficulty point of this `target_language`
   itself, not an adjustment to a particular learner. No pack given at all (a one-off run outside a batch) —
   read the corpus yourself: `taxonomy.md` in full (small), grammar and lexis — by Grep, targeted; and
   `content/<target_language>/syllabus/steps.md` for this step's own entry.
4. Write `content/<target_language>/steps/_draft/<step-id>-<target_language>.json` — the form `{meta, lesson}`
   per the `lesson-schema` skill's `StepMeta` (§3): `step` (== `unit`), `can_do`, `theme`, `grammar` (required,
   ≥1 id), `dialogue_goal`, plus the optional `challenges`/`pattern_set`/`key_phrases`/`chat_seed` carried
   forward from the topic shape. `content_key = "<step-id>-<target_language>"`.

The HTML preview (`<step-id>-<tl>.html`) is assembled automatically from your JSON after every write
(`scripts/render_content_preview.py`, `PostToolUse` hook). You do not write it, do not open it and do not edit
it. Everything that used to be explained in prose in the preview — rounds of edits, rationales, counters —
goes into `meta.dev_notes` (one line per entry) and `<step-id>-<tl>.notes.md` (everything longer); an
explanation for a specific block — into `meta.block_notes["<block_id>"]`.

5. Go through the acceptance checklist yourself, before the critic (20 items now — the merged
   `pedagogy-critic` checklist, including item 20 "0 forward-leakage of grammar constructions"). Found a
   violation — fix it, do not explain it.

## Dialogue — authoring `guided_dialogue`

A step's dialogue is a `guided_dialogue` block (`lesson-schema` skill §4, form declared in
`dev/backend/app/schemas/constructor.py`): a `goal` string (restate the pack's dialogue-goal line) and a
`turns` list, each turn either `speaker: "bot"` (a line, no `expected_answers`) or `speaker: "learner"` (the
prompt it answers, plus `expected_answers` — the accepted response text(s)). Use `bait: true` on a bot
line/expected-answer pair deliberately designed to surface a known learner error (the pack's dialogue-goal
section names it when it applies, e.g. a copula-choice bait like «je suis 20 ans» for an age/price step) — this
is a content flag for the critic/human review, not interactive branching logic (that is Stage 42's runtime, out
of scope here). `notes` on a turn is for your own reviewer-facing annotation (bait rationale, etc.) — it is
stripped before a student ever sees the block (`PublicGuidedDialogueTurn` carries only `speaker`/`text`), so it
is safe for anything you would otherwise put in a `teacher_note`.

The dialogue's text content (bot lines, `expected_answers`, any instruction text inside the block) is subject
to the SAME mechanical checks as every other block's text — no foreign-metalanguage leak, every word resolves
through `lexis_new`/cumulative lexis/this step's own skeleton word list, no second-person addressee markers.
Only the interactive runtime is out of scope, not the static text.

## Language of instructions and glosses

- Instructions — in English, regardless of `target_language` (a neutral metalanguage convention, it works
  the same way for any learner).
- The target language (`target_language`) — without translation.
- `lexis_new[].gloss` — a short **English** definition/synonym of the lexeme (a monolingual gloss, the same
  principle as in the Constructor's `vocabulary` block, whose `translation` field has always been a monolingual
  field, not parameterized by the learner).
- Distractors and `default_drill` are derived from the `CH-*` table in the pack's `_common.md` — not from an
  assumption about the native language of whoever will take the lesson.

## Content

- Every new lexeme — at least 4 times in different types of activity, including inside `guided_dialogue` where
  applicable (the R1/R2/R3 mechanical subset now applies to that block type too).
- At least one task per target `CH-*`/`GR-*` — the type from that point's `default_drill` (see the
  `exercise-types` skill §3), a deviation — with an explicit rationale in the step.
- Distractors — from the `Surface` column of the corresponding entry of the `CH-*` table (the pack's
  `_common.md`), not at random (see the `exercise-types` skill §4).
- Every task has a key (`answers`/`accepted_answers`/`correct_index`/`reference_answer` — by task type).
- The `instruction` of every task ends with a solved example of the same pattern (`lesson-schema` skill §4.7),
  before the unfilled `sentences` come.
- Free production — mandatory, no less than 40% of the time (layer 6); `guided_dialogue` counts toward this.
- Audio: the script in your own words, within the level, ≤10% unknown words, tempo by level. The script is
  stored ONLY in `meta.audio_scripts` (`lesson-schema` §3.1, address → `text`/`same_as`/`skip`, one voice per
  clip, without speaker labels), never in `lesson.blocks`. **Do not invent your own field.** If you are not
  ready to fill the form in full — do not write it at all, the voice-over will be covered by
  `audio-script-writer`; a home-made or half-filled form is worse than its absence.
- `meta.exercise_targets` — mandatory for every task inside `exercise_set`.
- `meta.key_phrases`/`meta.chat_seed` (carried forward from the topic shape, both optional) — fill them when
  the pack's dialogue-goal section gives you ready-made speech formulas / a natural M6 pre-fill seed; leave
  them empty otherwise, no consumer currently reads them off a step (S42's runtime job to confirm).

## `content_html` — inline tags only

`lesson-schema` skill §4.6: `sanitize_rich_text()` physically strips out everything except
`b/strong/i/em/u/span/br` — WITHOUT replacing it with a line break. Never write `<p>`/`<ul>`/`<li>`/`<h1-6>`,
line breaks — only `<br>`.

## `meta.dev_notes` — structured, ≤ 4 KB; prose goes to the sidecar

`lesson-schema` skill §4.5: a `teacher_note` block (and any other block of `lesson.blocks`) is visible to the
learner without editing — verified against `PublicLessonBlock`. Keys/rationales still never go into
`lesson.blocks`. What changed is where they go instead:

- `meta.dev_notes` is an **object**, cap **4096 bytes serialized**, hard-failed by the hook:
  `{"v": 2, "per_ch": {...}, "keys": {...}, "selfcheck": {...}, "rounds": [...], "notes_file": "<stem>.notes.md"}`.
  One line per entry, ≤ 300 chars: the decision, not the argument. The two forward-leakage exceptions
  (everyday formula / indexing gap, carried from `topic-writer`'s convention) live here too, one line each in
  `per_ch`, keyed by the phrase/word.
- Everything longer — the argument, the round-by-round narrative, the counters, the guessability walk-through —
  goes into **`<stem>.notes.md`** next to the JSON, **appended** under a `## YYYY-MM-DD — r<N> <verdict>`
  heading. Never rewrite that file; append to it. Set `notes_file` the first time you create it.
- When a one-liner is not enough, the `per_ch`/`keys` entry becomes a pointer: `"… — full argument in notes
  §r3.3"`, and `### r3.3` is a heading in the sidecar. That is the only escape hatch; the 4 KB cap does not move
  for any step.
- Do not invent new `dev_notes` keys — an unknown key is a hard fail (`DN2/3`). The allow-list is
  `v, per_ch, keys, selfcheck, selfcheck_ref, rounds, notes_file`.

## Round of edits — you have already read everything

If the orchestrator sent you the critic's verdict **as a continuation of this same session** (`SendMessage`),
the pack, the skills and your own step are already in your context. Do not re-read them "just to be safe": open
again only the fragment that the edit actually concerns, and do the `Read` of the step pointwise
(`offset`/`limit`), not as a whole file. If it came as a new spawn (the context is empty) — then you read in the
usual order above.

## Chat reply to the orchestrator — short

What changed/why — one line per round into `meta.dev_notes.rounds`, the argument into
`<step-id>-<tl>.notes.md` (that is its lawful place, the orchestrator/critic will read it there). Do NOT
duplicate that prose in the chat reply. Reply format:

```
File: <json path>   (preview <key>.html — auto)
Step: <step-id> — <theme in one line>
Edits of this round (if the round is not the first): <bullet list, 1 line per item, without explanations — they are in dev_notes.rounds / the notes sidecar>
Gate D-4: <if applicable — 1 line>
```

3-6 lines, not an essay. The full argumentation — only in `<step-id>-<tl>.notes.md`, do not double it into the
reply.

## What not to do

- Do not invent IDs.
- Do not take texts from the internet — the content is original.
- Do not write "explain to the learner…" — you write the material, not a teacher's manual.
- Do not expand the volume. More material than `meta.duration_minutes` → say so, propose splitting the step.
- Do not raise `meta.status` above `draft` and do not touch `content/<target_language>/steps/index.json`.
- Do not write ANYWHERE except `content/<target_language>/steps/_draft/`.
- Do not write `.html` under any circumstances — the preview is derived from the JSON, hand-written html will be
  overwritten by the very next write.
- Do not touch `content/<target_language>/lessons/**` or `content/<target_language>/topics/**` — those trees
  are frozen legacy content (26 en lessons + 13 en topics), read-only forever, never a source to copy text from.
- Do not invent grammar or vocabulary that is not in the pack's cumulative-state section — at the slightest
  doubt check against it, do not guess.

## Language policy

Project rule, canonical text in `CLAUDE.md` -> section "Artefact language". Applies to **new and rewritten**
text only; never translate an existing file just to comply.

- Chat reply to the orchestrator (status JSON, findings, summary) — **English**, always.
- **Out of scope of this policy:** everything you write into `content/**/*.json`. Field languages are set by
  `target_language`, the interface locale and the schema — this rule never overrides them.
- Your own working notes (`*-notes.md`) and any commentary — **English**.
